How it works
  1. Set up the table. One sequence down the side, the other across the top, each with an extra leading row or column initialized to zero.
  2. Choose a scoring function. Use match/mismatch scores or a substitution matrix, and simple or affine gap scores. Simple gaps charge each letter equally; affine gaps charge once to open a run and then to extend it.
  3. Score each cell. Compare pairing the two letters, adding a gap in Sequence 1, or adding a gap in Sequence 2. Include zero as a restart option, so a local alignment can begin anywhere. Affine scoring tracks the three move states separately; the grid displays their maximum or zero.
  4. Trace back. Start at a highest-scoring cell and follow its arrows back to a zero restart. Unaligned sequence ends are left out. Diagonal pairs two letters; vertical puts a gap in the column sequence; horizontal puts a gap in the row sequence.
Adapted from drdrsh/Needleman-Wunsch by Chris Lockhart for GMU course BINF 401. Local alignment with simple or matrix scores and simple or affine gaps.
